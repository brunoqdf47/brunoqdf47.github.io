02

Why I built it

A high-volume inbox has a hidden cost: not the reading, but the sorting. Which of these forty messages actually need me? Which am I just cc'd on? What's genuinely urgent versus loud? I was doing that triage by hand, every morning, and it was eating the first hour of my day. So I handed the judgement to an agent and the busywork to a flow.

03

How it's built — three parts

A clean separation: one part orchestrates, one part thinks, one part acts. Each can be changed without breaking the others.

Orchestration

Power Automate

Triggers on every incoming email, sets up the variables, calls the agent, then runs the conditional logic that decides what happens next. The conductor — it holds the flow together but makes no judgement of its own.

The brain

Copilot Studio agent

A custom agent I designed. It reads the raw email and returns a structured verdict — a one-line summary, the action required, an urgency level, and any deadline — so the flow has something clean to branch on.

The hands

Outlook · To-Do · Teams

The flow turns the agent's verdict into real actions: an Outlook category and flag, a move to the right folder, a To-Do task with the deadline, and — only when it's urgent and mine — a Teams alert.

04

The brain — what the agent extracts

The whole system hinges on turning a messy email into four clean fields. That's the agent's only job, and it's prompted to do exactly that — nothing more.

Structured output, every time
SUMMARY
One line, ~80 characters — what this email is actually about.
ACTION
What I specifically need to do, or "None" if I'm only cc'd.
URGENCY
HIGH · MEDIUM · LOW — judged by clear rules, not vibes.
DEADLINE
Any date mentioned, or "None" — feeds straight into the task.
HIGHWords like "urgent", "today", "ASAP", a VIP sender, or a deadline under 24h.
MEDIUMA direct request to me with a deadline 2–7 days out.
LOWFYI, informational, cc'd, no deadline.
05

The principle underneath

The interesting part isn't that an AI can read an email. It's the system around it — the trigger, the routing rules, the actions, the one place urgency is decided — that turns a clever model call into something that quietly runs every day without me.

I built this for my own inbox, but it's the same pattern I apply at work: let the agent judge, let the flow act, and keep the logic in one place you can change. It's the lived version of the "deliver & automate" stage in my method.
